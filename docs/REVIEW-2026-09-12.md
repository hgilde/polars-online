## Patterns

Six findings recur across models. A fix should land in every instance,
and a test written for one should be run against the others.

- **A. A live accumulator read inside a windowed path.** The windowed
  view is built and then one read goes to `self.…` instead: `ewridge`'s
  standardized solve (C1), `ew_class`'s `full`-shape factor (C12),
  `ew_cov`'s `mahal` and PCA refresh (C14), `marginal`'s lag ring (C18).
  Grep for `self.cov`, `self.classes`, `self.lag` inside any function that
  has a `view`, a `boundary()` or a `cut` in scope -- and for any `&self`
  method called from `read` or `pair`, since those take the view as an
  argument and the method does not.
- **B. Centring without an intercept.** With `standardize` on and
  `add_intercept = false`, `lasso` (C8), `kalman` (C10), `robust` (C11)
  and `sgd` (C13) centre the features anyway; `ewridge` alone scales by
  the raw second moment. Four wrong, one right; the fix is the same line
  in each.
- **C. A zero-weight row treated differently from a null target.**
  `lasso`'s selection has no `0/0` guard (C7); `kalman` (S9) and `robust`
  (S13) decay the per-target residual weight on a null target but not on
  a zero weight. `ewridge` gets both right and is the reference; so do
  `sgd`, whose two arms fall through to the same `continue`, and
  `marginal`, whose `learn` takes a `lam = 0` zero-weight row to
  `W_t = 0` on purpose.
- **D. Which `n_eff` a windowed model reports.** `ewridge` reports the
  in-window weight; `lasso` (C9) and `ew_class` (S14) report the whole
  history while fitting on the window; `ew_cov` (S15) emits the in-window
  weight and its accessor returns the whole history; `marginal` (S17)
  does the exact reverse of `ew_cov` -- accessor windowed, emitted field
  live. Five models, three conventions, one option. And the bank layer is
  a sixth instance (S19): `gram_of` reads the live accumulator for every
  kind, so `gram()` and a closed row carry the whole history beside a
  `coef` solved on the window, while `Bank::coef` and the closed row's
  `n_eff` go through each model's accessor -- three conventions in one
  column. Hard rule 8 should say which.
- **E. Re-centring through the raw second moment.** `E[x²] − m²` at a
  large offset destroys the variance, which is why `EwCov` went to
  Welford updates; four places still compute it: `TargetMoments::blend`
  (C16), `EwRidge::blend_toward_long_run` (C16, V9 confirmed),
  `window::truncated` (C17) and `marginal::cut` (C17). Each is a
  weight-respecting combination -- or its inverse -- of two sets of
  moments, and the centred identity for both directions is one line:
  `W·C = W_a·C_a + W_b·C_b + (W_a·W_b/W)·(m_a − m_b)(m_a − m_b)ᵀ`.
  Solve it for `C` to blend, for `C_b` to truncate. Nothing in it is
  larger than a centred moment or a difference of means.
- **F. Refused rather than ignored, applied to half the list.**
  `Spec::validate` states the rule in its own comments and applies it to
  `conformal_rate` without `conformal`, `window_every` without `window`,
  `pca_every` without `pca`, a residual flag on a model with no
  residuals, and `seqtest` with a `weight` or a `halflife`. It does not
  apply it to `drift_action = "reset"`, `drift_delta` or `drift_threshold`
  without `emit_drift` (S22 -- the detector is built only under the flag,
  so the reset never fires), `average_eta` without `emit_averaged`,
  `resid_autocorr_lag` without `emit_autocorr`, `long_halflife` without
  `session_shrink`, `session_gap` without `session`, `session_shrink`
  beside `session_gap = "reset"` or `group_close = "session"` (a blend
  into a state that is about to be emptied), `halflife` beside a
  different `level_halflife` on `holt` (S22), `ridge_decay` beside
  `session_shrink` (C6), `window` beside `mahal`/`pca` (C14) or beside
  `lags` on `marginal` (C18), and `hazard_col`/`exog_tvtp` beside a
  `targets` that does not name them (C20). The rule is the right one; the
  fix is to finish the list, and to put each refusal in the layer that
  owns the fact (the model's `Cfg::validate` where one exists,
  `Spec::validate` otherwise) so the CLI, the bank and the plugin get one
  message. Pass 8 found the same shape one level up: the check is applied
  at six entry points at three depths (S25), and the Python layer keeps a
  list of its own -- which parameters may be infinite -- that disagrees
  with `validate`'s in both directions (S27).

## Second opinions

`tests/test_river.py` is the model: `pytest.importorskip`, an **exact**
tier where the two libraries implement the same published recursion, and
a **statistical** tier where they differ by design. The proposal is one
new file, `tests/test_second_opinion.py`, with `sklearn`, `statsmodels`,
`scipy`, `filterpy`, `pandas` and `river` as optional dev dependencies,
and the mappings below -- each worked out from the objective the model
states in its module doc, so the *exact* tier really is exact.
`docs/TESTING.md` section B is where they belong, as T-S1..T-S14.

The one identity everything rests on: our accumulators are **mean-form**
(`S = Σ w zz' / W`, `r = Σ w z y / W`, `W = Σ w`), so a penalty `ridge`
on `S` is a penalty `ridge · W` on the sum-scale normal equations every
library solves. With a halflife, the row weights are `w_t · lam^(age_t)`
with `lam = 0.5^(1/halflife)` on a row-count clock; with a `window`, the
rows older than it are dropped before the weights are formed.

- **T-S1, `ew_ridge`, `standardize = false` -- `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge`.**
  `Ridge(alpha = ridge * W, fit_intercept = add_intercept).fit(X, y,
  sample_weight = w)`. sklearn centres `X` and `y` by the weighted means
  and leaves the intercept unpenalized, which is what `D = I minus the
  intercept slot` does; the two solutions are the same to rounding
  (`atol 1e-9` on standard-normal features). Exact, every row where our
  `solve_every` fired (`max_rows_between_solves = 1` to compare every
  row). `statsmodels.WLS` is the same check with `ridge = 0`.
- **T-S2, `ew_ridge`, `standardize = true` -- `Ridge` on standardized X.**
  `Xs = (X - mean_w(X)) / sd_w(X)` with the weighted moments;
  `Ridge(alpha = ridge * W, fit_intercept = True).fit(Xs, y,
  sample_weight = w)`; `coef / sd_w` are our slopes and `intercept -
  Σ coef·mean_w/sd_w` our intercept. Exact. Without an intercept, `Xs =
  X / sqrt(E_w[x²])` and `fit_intercept = False` -- the no-intercept
  branch scales by the raw second moment, and this is the check that
  pins pattern B for every model.
- **T-S3, `lasso` -- `sklearn.linear_model.ElasticNet`.** On `Xs` as in
  T-S2, `ElasticNet(alpha = lam, l1_ratio, fit_intercept = add_intercept,
  tol = 1e-12, max_iter = 10_000).fit(Xs, y, sample_weight = w)`. sklearn's
  objective is `(1/(2W)) Σ w (y - Xs b)² + α ρ |b|₁ + ½ α (1-ρ) |b|²`,
  which is our `½ b'Cb - d'b + l1 |b|₁ + ½ l2 |b|²` with `C = corr`, `d =
  cov(xs, y)`, `l1 = lam ρ`, `l2 = lam (1-ρ)`, up to a constant. Exact for
  every path point; unscale as in T-S2.
- **T-S4, `huber` and `quantile` -- `sklearn.linear_model.HuberRegressor`,
  `statsmodels.QuantReg`.** Statistical tier: our IRLS-on-the-prior-
  residual is not their algorithm. Exact tier exists at the limits:
  `huber_delta = 1e9` is T-S1 with `ridge = 0`; `quantile` at `tau =
  0.5` with `halflife = inf` converges to `QuantReg(y, X).fit(q = 0.5)`
  within the IRLS floor `quantile_eps`. For pattern B (C11) the limit
  case is enough: `huber_delta = 1e9, add_intercept = False, standardize
  = True` must equal `LinearRegression(fit_intercept = False)`.
- **T-S5, `kalman`, `standardize = false` -- `filterpy.kalman.KalmanFilter`.**
  `dim_x = k_total, dim_z = 1, F = I, H = z_t (row), Q = diag(q) · d_clock,
  R = obs_var / w, P = p0 · I, x = 0`, `predict()` then `update(y)` per
  row; with `revert_halflife` finite, `F = diag(2^(-d/r_i))` and `Q` added
  after `predict`. `kf.x` is our `beta`, `kf.P` our `P`, `H P Hᵀ + R`
  our `pred_var`, to rounding (Joseph form vs simple form differ in the
  last bits; `rtol 1e-9`). Exact. `pykalman` is the same check with the
  batch filter.
- **T-S6, `ew_class` -- `sklearn.naive_bayes.GaussianNB`,
  `discriminant_analysis`, `scipy.stats.multivariate_normal`.**
  `halflife = inf` makes every in-window weight 1. `diagonal`:
  `GaussianNB(var_smoothing = 0).fit(X, y)` uses population variance and
  matches `precision_prior → 0` to rounding on `predict_proba`. `full`
  and `shared`: sklearn's `QuadraticDiscriminantAnalysis` divides by
  `n - 1`, so compare the density instead -- `multivariate_normal(mean =
  class mean, cov = class cov + r_c I).logpdf(x)` plus `ln π_c`, softmax,
  against our posteriors; `scipy` is the second opinion on the Gaussian,
  sklearn's `_class_means` on the moments. Exact. Under a `window` fit
  them on the in-window rows only.
- **T-S7, `sgd`, `squared` loss -- `sklearn.linear_model.SGDRegressor`.**
  Statistical tier only: sklearn's `partial_fit` standardizes against
  the moments from *before* the row (the order `SgdCfg::scale_features`
  says it replaced), and its `invscaling` schedule counts rows, not
  `n_eff`. Both converge to the same point, so `coef_` and ours agree to
  `atol 1e-2` at 20 000 rows of unit-scale features, and that is enough
  for pattern B (C13): `SGDRegressor(fit_intercept = False, penalty =
  None)` on `X / sqrt(E[x²])`, unscaled, against `add_intercept = false,
  scale_features = true`. The exact check that does not need sklearn is
  `dot(coef, x) == pred`, which every other model meets.
- **T-S8, `ew_cov` under a `window` -- `numpy`.** The windowed moments are
  a weighted mean and covariance of the in-window rows with weights
  `lam^age`, which `numpy.average` and `numpy.cov(aweights = ..,
  ddof = 0)` give directly; `scipy.spatial.distance.mahalanobis(x, mean,
  inv(cov + prior·scale·I))` for `mahal` and `numpy.linalg.eigh` for
  `pc<j>_var`. Exact to `1e-9`. The Rust test `a_window_reports_exactly_
  the_rows_inside_it` already does this for `mean`, `var` and `cov` with
  its own `direct_window` oracle; C14 is the two slots it does not cover.
- **T-S9, every EW accumulator -- `pandas.DataFrame.ewm`.** This is the
  one that should have been first: pandas implements the *same*
  mean-form, adjusted, centred EW recursion. `df.ewm(halflife = h, adjust
  = True, ignore_na = True)` and then `.mean()`, `.var(bias = True)`,
  `.cov(bias = True)`, `.corr()` are `ew_cov`'s `mean`, `var`, `cov`,
  `corr` on a row-count clock **to rounding, at any offset** -- pandas
  keeps `mean_x`, `mean_y` and a centred `cov` and updates them Welford-
  style, so it is a second opinion on the *numerics*, not just the
  algebra. With an irregular clock, `df.ewm(halflife = pd.Timedelta(..),
  times = df["clock"])` is the clock model (`adjust = True` is forced
  there, which is ours). Row weights (`w` on the `step`) are the one
  thing pandas' `ewm` lacks; use `w ≡ 1` or T-S10. The same call on the
  two columns `[x_j, y_t]` is `marginal`'s pair `(j, t)`, and on `y_t`
  alone it is `TargetMoments`. `bias = False` is Kish's correction and
  must **not** be used: it is `n_kish`, not `n_eff`. Exact tier, `rtol
  1e-12` at offset 0 and `1e-9` at offset `1e8` -- the second number is
  the one pattern E fails.
- **T-S10, any windowed or blended accumulator -- `numpy` with explicit
  weights.** Two facts make one oracle serve every site of pattern E.
  (i) A `window` keeps the rows with `age < window` at weight `w ·
  lam^age`: `numpy.cov(X[keep].T, aweights = w_keep, ddof = 0)` and
  `numpy.average(X[keep], weights = w_keep, axis = 0)` are the truncated
  co-moments and means. (ii) A `session_shrink` blend at `f` of a fast
  accumulator (halflife `h`) with its slow twin (halflife `H`) that saw
  the **same rows** is the accumulator with row weight `w · ((1−f) ·
  lam_h^age + f · lam_H^age)` -- write out `af·m_f + as·m_s` with the
  mean-form definitions and the `W'` cancels -- so the same two `numpy`
  calls with that weight vector are the blended moments, exactly, before
  any solve. Both are two-pass (centre, then square), so both are
  correct at any offset. Exact tier, `rtol 1e-9`.
- **T-S11, `marginal`'s `t`, `n_serial`, `t_serial` -- `scipy.stats`,
  `statsmodels`.** At `halflife = inf`, unit weights: `scipy.stats.
  pearsonr(x, y)` is `corr` exactly, and its `statistic` transformed by
  `r·sqrt((n−2)/(1−r²))` is `t` with `n_kish = n` (exact). `statsmodels.
  tsa.stattools.acf(x, nlags = L, adjusted = False)` is `lagcorr_xx` in
  the **statistical** tier only -- it centres every lag at the overall
  mean and divides by the lag-0 variance, where ours centres each leg at
  the pre-row mean; they agree to `atol 0.02` at 5 000 rows. The Bartlett
  factor itself has an exact reference: the `"truncated"` rule is the
  ratio of a long-run variance to a variance under **uniform** kernel
  weights, `statsmodels.stats.sandwich_covariance.cov_hac(...,
  weights_func = weights_uniform, nlags = L)` over `cov_hac(nlags = 0)`
  on the product series `x_t·y_t` (independent series, so the product's
  autocovariance is the product of the two); S18 is what that comparison
  exposes. `weights_bartlett` is the kernel that would fix it.
- **T-S12, `marginal`'s bins and split -- `scipy.stats.binned_statistic`,
  `sklearn.tree`.** With given edges, `halflife = inf`, unit weights:
  `binned_statistic(x, y, statistic = "mean", bins = edges)` is
  `bin_mean_y` and `statistic = "count"` is `bin_n`, exact (`np.var` per
  bin for `bin_var_y`, `ddof = 0`). `DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth =
  1).fit(x[:, None], y)` chooses its threshold among *all* midpoints and
  ours among the edges, so its impurity decrease over `var(y)` is an
  upper bound on `split_gain`, with equality when `tree_.threshold[0]`
  falls between the same two data points as `split_at`. The existing
  Rust test recomputes the gain by hand over the same edges; the tree is
  the check that the hand recomputation is the right quantity.
- **T-S13, `emit_averaged` -- `river`'s `EWARegressor`, and `numpy`.** The
  exponentially weighted average forecaster weights slot `i` by
  `exp(−η · L_i)` with `L_i` the *cumulative* squared loss; ours by
  `exp(−η · σ_i²)` with `σ²` the EW *mean* squared residual. Statistical
  tier: over the same slot predictions at `halflife = inf` the two
  `pred_averaged` columns start together and part, since theirs sharpens
  and ours does not (S23). The exact check needs no library: from the
  bank's own `emit_sigma` columns, `pred_averaged == Σ softmax(−η ·
  sigma²) · pred` over the slots, `rtol 1e-12`.
- **T-S14, `holt` -- `statsmodels.tsa.holtwinters.Holt`.** On a row-count
  clock with unit weights, `Holt(y).fit(smoothing_level = 1 −
  0.5^(1/h_level), smoothing_trend = 1 − 0.5^(1/h_trend), optimized =
  False)` is the same level/trend recursion once the mean-form warm-up
  has washed out (statistical tier, `atol 1e-6` after `10·h` rows;
  exact if `holt.rs`, still unread under 6d, is the textbook update). It
  is the second opinion S22's `holt` item needs: it says which halflife
  the level actually ran at.

## Findings

Prefix: **C** changes a number or a state; **S** a semantic inconsistency
between two places that both look intentional; **P** performance against
a stated invariant; **D** documentation; **V** something a pass could not
confirm and the next one should.

### C1. `solve_standardized` reads the live accumulators under a `window`

`crates/online-core/src/ewridge.rs`, `solve_standardized`, the
`add_intercept` branch. Pattern A.

`solve()` passes the truncated `cov` it got from `view()`, and the
no-intercept branch reads it. The intercept branch reads `self.cov`:

```rust
let means: Vec<f64> = zidx.iter().map(|&z| self.cov.mean(z)).collect();
...
c[i * kf + j] = self.cov.cov(zidx[i + 1], zidx[j + 1]);
```

while `keep` in the same branch reads `cov.raw(...)`. So with `window` +
`standardize` + `add_intercept` the centred Gram and the means are the
**full** history and the right-hand side (`b`, `ybar`) is the windowed
one. The coefficients come from two different histories and the window's

### C2. `view()` falls back to the live state when the window is empty

`crates/online-core/src/ewridge.rs`, `view`, `n_eff`, `sigma2`, `solve`;
**the same code in `lasso.rs`** (`Lasso::view`, `?` on `truncated_mean`).

`view()` uses `?` on `truncated(..)` and on `truncated_mean(..)` for every
target. Both return `None` when the in-window weight is `<= 0`. `view()`
turns that into `None`, and every caller treats `None` as "nothing has aged
out yet, read the live state" -- the same value the early return for
`old.cov.w == 0.0` gives. The two situations are opposites.

When it happens:

- a target that was null on every row inside the window -- a sparse
  long-horizon label beside a dense one -- makes the whole view `None`, so
  **every** target is solved on the unwindowed Gram and `n_eff` reports the
  unwindowed weight (so `min_periods` passes). It flips back when the
  target reappears;
- a clock gap at or beyond the window;
- a tiny negative from rounding in `w_now - f * old.w`.

`window.rs`'s `trim` comment says the model "reports an empty window rather
than stale numbers"; `ewridge` does the reverse. Note also that
`predict` gates a slot on the *untruncated* `self.wj[j] > 0.0`, and that
`step`'s first-solve clause reads `self.cov.n_eff()` (untruncated) where
`predict` reads `self.n_eff()` (truncated).

**`ew_class::view` is the model to copy**: a class whose `truncated`
comes back `None` is replaced by an empty accumulator ("scores nothing
rather than scoring on rows the window excludes") and the view is still
returned. **`ew_cov::view` is the other one** (pass 5): its `match` on
`truncated` has the three-way answer -- borrow when nothing has aged
out, own the truncation, and on `None` own an *emptied* copy
(`set_moments` to zero, "report an empty state rather than dividing by
it") -- which is exactly the shape `ewridge` and `lasso` need.
`marginal::pair` (pass 6) is the per-target version: a `cut` that comes
back `None` reports `n_eff = 0` and NaN for that pair and leaves every
other target alone, which is C2's fix in miniature.

Fix: make `view()` return a three-way answer (live / truncated / empty),
handle the empty case per target (that target's slots NaN, its `r`
untouched) rather than for the model, and clamp the truncated weight at 0
before the test.

**Test.** Rust, two targets, `window = 60`, halflife 25, `y1` null from

and must be rewritten with the fix.

The exact centred mixture has no cancellation: `var = a·var + b·var' +

= m_f − m_s`. It is the same `a`, `b` and means the code already has.

### C17. `window::truncated` and `marginal::cut` re-centre through the raw second moment

`crates/online-core/src/window.rs`, `truncated`:

```rust
// Back to raw second moments, subtract, and re-centre on the
// window's own mean.
let now = w_now * (cov.comoments()[i * k + j] + cov.mean(i) * cov.mean(j));
let then = old.w * (old.c[i * k + j] + old.m[i] * old.m[j]);
cen[i * k + j] = (now - f * then) / w - mean[i] * mean[j];
```

and `crates/online-core/src/marginal.rs`, `cut`, the same three lines
one pair at a time (`raw / wn - a * b`). Pattern E, and the one that
matters most: `truncated` is the shared truncation **every** windowed
model calls -- `ewridge` (through `view`, and so every windowed solve,
`sigma2` aside), `lasso`, `ew_class`, `ew_cov` -- and `cut` is
`marginal`'s own copy. Its doc says the subtraction "is a difference of
positives, so it loses precision in proportion to the fraction
discarded"; that is true and is not the problem. The problem is the
round trip through `E[x²]`, which at offset `1e8` and unit variance
leaves the windowed variance with a resolution of about `2` (the terms
are `~2e17`, ulp `32`, divided by `w ≈ 18`), i.e. nothing; at `1e7`
about `1%`; at `1e6` about `1e-4`. Every existing window test runs at
offset `0` (`ewridge`, `marginal`) or `100` (`ew_cov`, `x ∈ [100, 110]`,
tolerance `1e-8` relative, which passes at that level and not far
above). So a windowed `ew_cov` on a price, a windowed `marginal` pair
against a price-level target, a windowed `standardize` fit on a level
feature, and a windowed `ew_class` on level features are all wrong today,
silently, by an amount that grows as the square of the level.

Fix: the centred truncation. With `W` the live weight, `W_u' = f·W_u` the
decayed snapshot weight and `W_R = W − W_u'`:

```text
m_R   = (W·m − W_u'·m_u) / W_R                       (as now: a mean, fine)
W_R·C_R = W·C − W_u'·C_u − (W_u'·W / W_R)·(m_u − m)(m_u − m)ᵀ
```

(the pooling identity of pattern E solved for the remainder; `(m_u − m)`
is a difference of two means, order `σ`, at any offset). `cut` is the
same with `(m_u,x − m_x)(m_u,y − m_y)` for the cross term. This also
closes **V3**: `cen[i,i]` today is not floored, and with the centred
form a slightly negative diagonal can only come from the "difference of
positives" loss the doc already accepts, so `.max(0.0)` on the diagonal
is then the right floor.

**Test.** Library first, T-S10 (i): `numpy.cov(X[keep].T, aweights =
lam^age, ddof = 0)` on rows at `x + 1e8` against `ew_cov`'s windowed

### S18. `marginal`'s `"truncated"` serial rule floors the factor at `f64::MIN_POSITIVE` and uses no kernel

`marginal.rs`, `serial_factor`, `SerialRule::Truncated`: `(1.0 + 2.0 *
sum).max(f64::MIN_POSITIVE)`. A negatively autocorrelated pair -- two
mean-reverting series, `rho_x(1) = rho_y(1) = −0.8` -- gives `1 + 2·0.64
= 2.28` (fine), but `rho_x(1) = 0.8, rho_y(1) = −0.8` gives `1 − 1.28 <
0`, the floor fires, and `n_serial = n_kish / 5e-324 = +inf`, `t_serial
= ±inf` (or NaN at `corr = 0`, from `0 · inf`). A factor at or below zero
is an estimate outside the parameter space, and `Geometric` already
answers that case with NaN ("say nothing rather than a negative count");
`Truncated` should do the same rather than report infinite evidence.
The cause is the missing kernel: a truncated sum of `rho_x·rho_y` with
unit weights is not guaranteed positive, which is the whole reason
Newey-West uses Bartlett weights `1 − l/(L+1)` (positive semi-definite
by construction). With those weights the rule is the standard HAC
correction, and a third rule, or a `weights` option on this one, would
give the user the estimator every econometrics library ships.

**Test.** Library first, T-S11: `statsmodels.stats.sandwich_covariance.
cov_hac` on the product series `x_t·y_t` with `weights_func =
weights_uniform, nlags = L` over `nlags = 0` reproduces our factor to
`rtol 1e-9` at `halflife = inf` (exact: both are the same unweighted
sum over the same sample autocovariances, up to statsmodels' `n`
divisors, which cancel in the ratio) -- and goes to zero or negative on
the mixed-sign pair where ours goes to `MIN_POSITIVE`; with
`weights_bartlett` it stays positive, which is the fix's reference.
Rust: `serial_factor(Truncated, &[1], &[0.8], &[-0.8])` must return NaN,
not `(5e-324, ..)`; `t_serial` on that pair NaN, not `±inf`.

### S23. `emit_averaged`'s weights are in target units², so `average_eta = 1` is one regime for a price and another for a return

the average can silently be over one slot. Either make `eta` scale-free

number") and `marginal` bounds its bins the same way; the ring, which is
larger than either in every realistic configuration, is the one
allocation without a bound. `window_every` is the lever the user has,
and no validator tells them when to pull it.

**Test.** `EwRidgeCfg::validate` with `window / (typical d_clock)` rows
of `k²` floats over a budget: refuse, naming the number and
`window_every`. The typical `d_clock` is not known at validation --
`min_rows` from the bank's clock schedule, or a documented assumption

### P5. The closed-row queue is saved with the bank and has no bound

`bank.rs`, `queue_closed`, `to_file`; `_bank.py`, `closed_groups`,
`fit_predict_batches`. "A bank's memory must not depend on the caller
polling" is true of the streams and false of the queue: a driver that
never drains keeps every closed row, and `save` writes them all. A
closed `marginal` row carries `p·T` `PairRow`s with up to eight lists
each -- at `p = 6 000, T = 4, lags = [1, 2, 5]` and bins, a few MB per
row -- and `group_close = "session"` produces one per group per session,
so a thousand groups over a year of sessions is a state file in the
hundreds of GB with nothing having gone wrong. `runner.rs` drains to the
sidecar (10b, to read); `fit_predict_batches` does not drain at all.
Either a documented cap with a refusal that names `closed_groups()`, or
`fit_predict_batches` yields the closed frame alongside each chunk.

**Test.** Numbers: `len(bank.save_bytes())` after 100 sessions of one
`marginal` group with `p = 1 000`, undrained, against drained. And a
refusal, if one is added.

### D3. `Kalman::coefficients` unscales with the current stats

`kalman.rs`. The betas live in the standardized coordinates of the stats
at the time they were learned; `coefficients()` reads them out with
today's scales and means. The tests acknowledge it ("locking its betas
into the standardized space of the first few rows") and route around it;
the method doc does not say it. `pred` and `coef` are consistent with
each other (both use the current stats), but "coefficient per unit of
`x`" drifts with the standardizer. One paragraph on the method.

### D4. Robust's scale is the plain EW residual variance

`robust.rs`: `s` in the Huber cut and the quantile floor is
`sqrt(sig2)`, the EW mean of squared raw residuals -- which the outliers
Huber exists to resist inflate. The usual choice is a robust scale (MAD
or a Huber-weighted variance). Not a bug; the module doc should say the
scale is not itself robust, since `huber_delta` is "in units of the EW
residual std" and a user will read that as a robust std. T-S4's
`HuberRegressor` (which estimates its scale jointly) is the second
opinion on how much it matters: on `test_river.py`'s 3 %-contaminated
stream the two slopes should be within `0.05`, not the `0.5` that test
allows today.

### D5. `sgd::coefficients` unscales with the current stats, and the step's stats are not quite the scaler's

`sgd.rs`. D3's paragraph applies verbatim: each step is taken in the
standardized coordinates of the moments as they stood on that row, and
`coefficients()`, `predict` and the constraint's projection read the
betas out with today's. The `scale_features` field doc says "unscaling
the coefficients on the way out so they stay in the caller's units" and
should carry D3's caveat. One wrinkle only `sgd` has: the step
standardizes against the moments *with the row admitted at unit weight*
(`including(lam)`) and then updates the scaler at the row's *actual*
weight, so for `weight ≠ 1` the coordinates the gradient was taken in
and the ones the projection and `coefficients()` use a moment later
differ by the row's weight. The doc explains why the prediction does
not depend on the weight; it does not say that the reported
coefficients then do (and that under a constraint the projection is
taken in the second system, not the first). Not a bug at `weight = 1`,
which is every test.

### D6. `window::truncated`'s precision note describes the wrong loss

`window.rs`: "In `f64` it is a difference of positives, so it loses
precision in proportion to the fraction discarded: negligible at `window
= 3·halflife`". True of the weight and the means; for the co-moments the
dominant loss is C17's, which is proportional to `(level/σ)²` and does
not care about the window length. After the fix the note is right as
written; until then it understates the damage by the square of the
offset. `a_windowed_fit_is_the_fit_of_the_rows_inside_the_window`
repeats the note in its tolerance comment ("about eight significant
figures") and would be wrong at any offset.
