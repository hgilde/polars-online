## Findings

### F3. `quantile` degraded toward the mean at short halflives

`crates/online-core/src/robust.rs`, `row_update` (N9, batch 6). The
warm-up kept every row a least-squares row until the *band's* weight
reached `3·k_total`. Under a finite halflife the band's weight cannot
grow past `p/(1 − λ)`, its share `p` of the effective sample, and at
`τ = 0.9` the band holds about 7% of rows: at `halflife = 50` it
saturates at 4.9 against a bar of 6, and the fit falls back into warm-up
-- least-squares rows, aimed at the conditional mean -- and out again.
Measured, second-half coverage at `τ = 0.9` on 20,000 rows of normal
noise: 0.825 at `halflife = 30`, 0.864 at 40, 0.885 at 50, 0.898 at 100
and 0.900 from 200 up; the median, whose band holds three times as much,
was exact at every halflife. The suite ran `quantile` at `halflife = inf`
or 2000 only. Better than the model N9 replaced, whose coverage read
0.777 at `halflife = 2000`, and not what the docs say the model does.

### F1. `quantile`'s per-target warm-up gate flickered under decay

The same field, the other role. `wj` became the band's weight in N9, and
`target_n_eff_into` reported it, so the per-target `min_periods` gate
(S2) compared against the band rather than the rows the target was
present on. First predictions were unaffected -- the model's own gate
makes every early row a least-squares row -- but once the band's weight
saturated below `min_periods` the gate closed again. Measured at
`τ = 0.9`, `halflife = 100`, `min_periods = 20`: predictions per thousand
rows of 178, 233, 2, 88, 155 and 203, where `huber` predicted every row.
A regression from the model before N9, whose `wj` was the reweighted
row sum, and three documents stated the old semantics: the changelog's S2
entry, `Spec::min_periods` in `spec.rs` and `docs/EXTENDING.md`. The
recommendation made to the user before implementing N9 said the gate
would stay on the rows present; the implementation did not do that.

**One cause, one fix (batch 7).** `wj` played two roles that N9 made
different quantities: the Hessian's mass, and the target's evidence.
`robust` keeps a per-target observation weight `wobs` now -- the rows the
target was present on at their raw weights, decayed -- which the S2 gate
and the quantile warm-up read, while `wj` stays the Gram's. And the band
is never narrower than `(k_total/n)^{2/5}` of `σ` for the target's
effective sample `n`, the smoothed-quantile bandwidth rate, which a long
stream leaves behind (`quantile_eps` is where it settles) and which keeps
the Hessian fed under a short halflife. `huber`'s gate reads `wobs` too,
where it read the reweighted sum: the docs' "rows it was present on" is
true of both now. The state gained a field: schema 10, no loader for 9.
Measured after: coverage 0.895 at `halflife = 30` and 0.896 at 40,
and every row predicted at `min_periods = 20`.

The gate then found what else the warm-up had been doing: rebuilding a
fit a row at the input bound had left behind. Such a row sets the Gram
and the cross-moment at its own scale, every later row is outside the
band, and the nudges -- `2h·ψ·z` over the band's weight -- cannot move
the fit until that weight has decayed to nothing, where each is a step
that outgrows the band; the fit oscillates, the weight underflows after
1400 halflives, and the prediction is withheld (the bounded-extremes
contract, at row 29,520 of its 30,520). A band holding under one row per
coefficient takes least-squares rows until it holds rows again: from one
row up an outside row's step lands inside the band, and the floor keeps a
settled band at a share of the sample, never near one row, so the bias
F3 removed does not return.

### F2. The runner's per-chunk drain lost closed rows on failure

`crates/online-polars/src/runner.rs`, `run_with` (P5, batch 3b). The bank's
closed groups were drained after every chunk and the sidecar published
only by a run that completed. A `run` that failed midway had drained the
earlier chunks' closed rows into a temporary that was then deleted, and
the bank no longer held them; before P5 a caller-owned bank still did,
and `run`'s doc still said the bank was "left as it was after the last
chunk it accepted". `po.run` and the CLI were unaffected, their bank being
dropped either way; the Python `fit_predict_batches` wrote in a `finally`,
so the two paths disagreed. **Fixed (batch 7):** the runner publishes the
sidecar however the run stops, when it drained anything -- a drained row's
only home is that file -- and the output keeps its whole-run rule.
