## Findings -- C24, after C23

### C24. `ftrl`'s halflife shrinks the coefficients toward zero, on every row and by a closed-form factor on a null one

`crates/online-core/src/ftrl.rs`, `step` (the decay block, `weight`),
`predict` (`weight_of(zz·λ, n·λ)`); `tests/reference.py`, `ftrl_ref`.
Read in full in pass 10 (6e).

The proximal weight is `b = −(z − sgn(z)·l1) / ((β + √n)/α + l2)`, a
closed form in two *sums*: `z = Σ (g_s − σ_s·b_s)` and `n = Σ g_s²`.
The decay multiplies both by `λ` per row ("so it forgets on the same
schedule", the module doc). But the closed form is not homogeneous in
`(z, n)`: the numerator scales by `λ`, the denominator by less -- `√n`
by `√λ`, and `β/α`, `l2` not at all -- so the weight after a decay is
`b·λ·D(n)/D(λn)` with `D(n) = (β + √n)/α + l2`, a factor strictly below
1 for `λ < 1`, between `λ` (where `β/α + l2` dominates) and `√λ` (where
`√n/α` does). Every other model here is either mean-form -- `S`, `r`,
`W` decay together and the coefficient does not move on a null row --
or update-form, where the state *is* the coefficient (`sgd`, `pa`,
`rls`, `kalman` without `revert_halflife`). `ftrl` is the one model
whose coefficient is a function of decayed sums, and the decay it
shares with the others means something else to it.

Three consequences, each a number the user reads:

- **A null-target row, a zero-weight row or a gap on the clock shrinks
  every coefficient.** One halflife of nothing takes a coefficient to
  between 50% and 71% of itself; three halflives to 12-35%.
  `predict(x, d_clock)` after a weekend on a time or volume clock
  returns coefficients at a fraction of what the last learned row left
  (2.25 → 0.75 after three halflives, in the intercept example below),
  and `pred` heads to `0` (squared) or `0.5` (logistic). A coordinate
  `l1` was holding just above zero is zeroed by the decay alone, since
  `|z|` falls under `l1`.
- **The fixed point is biased toward zero, in proportion to `1−λ`.**
  Discounting caps `Σλ^age` at `1/(1−λ)`, so a penalty fixed on the sum
  scale (`l1`, `l2`, `β`) is a *mean*-scale penalty of `(1−λ)` times
  itself -- the sum-vs-mean identity the second-opinions section opens
  with, applied where it hurts -- and the `√n` term stops telescoping
  (`Σλ^age·σ_s ≠ √n/α` under discounting), which adds a second bias of
  order `√((1−λ)·E[g²])/α`. Intercept-only squared loss on a constant
  target `μ`: `w* = μ/(1 + (1−λ)·K)`, and at `halflife = 100` with the
  defaults (`α = 0.1, β = 1, l2 = 1`) a target of 5 settles at **2.24**;
  at 1000 at 4.88; at 20 at 0.96. A slope of 1.5 on a unit-variance
  feature with unit noise fits as 1.25 at `h = 500` and **0.91** at
  `h = 100`. Logistic: a base rate of 0.9 is reported as 0.82 at `h =
  1000` and **0.69** at `h = 100` -- the intercept that
  `base_rate_is_learned_by_the_intercept` checks at `inf` is at a third
  of its value at a hundred rows. (All from a 30-line re-implementation
  of the module doc's recursion, which T-S19 turns into a test; nothing
  was run against the repo.)
- **Nothing in the crate or the suite sees it.** Every `ftrl` test but
  `forgets_on_the_clock` runs at `halflife = inf`, and that one asks
  only that a flipped rule take `p` past 0.4. T-R1 drives river's
  optimizer with the same gradients at `inf`. `reference.py`'s
  `ftrl_ref` discounts the same sums ("the decay is applied to `n` and
  `z` *before* the proximal weights are computed"), so agreement with it
  confirms the arithmetic and says nothing about the intent -- the
  relationship C16's test has to its oracle.

Whether this was ever intended: the module doc's "forgets on the same
schedule as every other model here" says no, and the behaviour it
produces exists elsewhere under a name -- `kalman`'s `revert_halflife`,
documented as reversion toward zero and shown in E41 to help only where
"the prior must match the world". If it is wanted, it is that knob and
should be called so.

Fix, in two parts. (1) Carry the discounted proximal sum as state: `D ←
λ·D + σ_t` beside `n` and `z`, and `b = −(z − sgn·l1)/(D + β/α + l2)`.
At `λ = 1` this is `√n/α` exactly (the telescoping), so the undecayed
model and T-R1 do not move; under decay it removes the `√n` bias, the
larger of the two (the re-implementation with it gives 4.65, 4.96 and
3.60 for the constant 5 at `h = 100, 1000, 20`, which is `μ/(1 +
(1−λ)(β/α + l2))` to three figures). (2) Decide what `l1`, `l2` and `β`
mean under a halflife. Left as sum-scale constants they are a
mean-scale ridge of `(1−λ)(β/α + l2)`, and the doc has to say so;
scaled by `W = Σλ^age` (already `w_sum`) they become the mean-scale
penalties every other model here takes, with `l2` meaning what
`ewridge.ridge` means (with `l2·W` the re-implementation lands on `5/(1
+ l2/E[x²]) = 2.5` at `l2 = 1`, the ridge fixed point). The first is
the bug fix and the second the design question. Short of either, the
doc must state the shrinkage factor, and send a user who wants
forgetting to `halflife = inf` with `session`/`reset`, or to `sgd` and
`ewridge`.

**Test.** Rust: `ftrl(loss = squared, halflife = 100)` on `x ≡ 0, y ≡
5` (intercept only), 20 000 rows: `pred` on the last row is `2.24`
today and must be `5.0 ± 1e-3`; the same at `halflife = inf` passes
today and is the control. The null-row check, exact: after 5 000 rows,
one row with `y = None` and `d_clock = 100`: `coef` moves by the factor
`λ·D(n)/D(λn) = 0.70` today, and must not move (`rtol 1e-12`), as
`ewridge`'s does not on the same row. Python: `ftrl(halflife = 1000,
loss = "logistic")` on a 0.9 base rate: `pred` on the last of 50 000
rows is `0.82` today, `0.90 ± 0.01` after. Second opinion: T-S19; river
at `inf` as the negative control.
